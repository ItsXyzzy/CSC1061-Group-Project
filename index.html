Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to book my train tickets/reservations far in advance?

For most high-speed trains (like TGV, Thalys, or Eurostar) and international/night trains, it is highly recommended to book your tickets and seat reservations as far in advance as possible (often up to 3-6 months ahead). This is essential to secure the lowest fares and guarantee a seat. For local or regional trains, booking on the day or shortly before is generally fine.

Should I buy individual tickets or a rail pass (like Eurail/Interrail)?

This depends on your itinerary. A rail pass is usually best if you plan on traveling to many different countries or taking several long-distance journeys within a short period, offering great flexibility. Individual tickets are often cheaper if you are only visiting one or two countries, have a fixed schedule, and book far in advance. Use our route planner tools to compare the total cost for your specific journey!

Is there a baggage limit on European trains?

Generally, no. One of the great advantages of rail travel is the lack of strict weight limits or extra fees for luggage, unlike flying. However, you must be able to lift and manage your own bags onto the train and store them in the overhead racks or designated luggage areas near your seat. We recommend bringing bags you can easily carry yourself.

Where can I store my luggage at the main European train stations?

Most major train stations across Europe offer luggage storage facilities, either in the form of secured lockers or a manned "Left Luggage" office. This is very convenient for day trips or exploring a city before checking into your accommodation. We recommend checking the station's official website in advance for specific locations, operating hours, and costs..

Are the long travel days (e.g. Paris to Madrid or Vienna to Krakow) manageable, and how can I maximize comfort?

While there are a few full-day high-speed journeys on this route (approximately 8-10 hours), they are highly manageable and part of the adventure! The key is preparation.

  • Choose comfortable seating options

    For these long legs, investing in a 1st Class ticket often provides wider seats, more legroom, quieter carriages, and sometimes complimentary amenities, making the journey feel much shorter.

  • Utilize Night Trains

    We highly recommend using the overnight services (like the ÖBB Nightjet) for routes such as Paris to Vienna. This saves you a day of travel time and a night of hotel costs. You also wake up in your destination with a full day for activities.

  • Stay Connected

    Pack power banks, download movies/podcasts, and ensure you have snacks and drinks. Most modern high-speed trains offer reliable Wi-Fi and power outlets, allowing you to work, relax, or simply watch the diverse European landscapes change outside your window.
